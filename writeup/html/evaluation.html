Each question is asked once through the production ask(), then judged three times

Over-abstentionabstained on an answerable questionDeterministic

Accuracy alone hides why an answer failed: groundedness separates “wrong” from “unsupported”; retrieval recall and the failure classes say whether to fix ingestion, retrieval or generation; safe / useful / over-abstention exposed the trade-off of the live safety gate (§4.3). Limits: groundedness judges against the text of a cited page, so a claim drawn from a figure printed on it reads as unsupported (§4.5); accuracy grades against one reference. Both judge prompts are verbatim in docs/PROMPTS.md §2. DeepEval 4.3.1: GEval correctness with the harness's 1 / 0.5 / 0 rubric, FaithfulnessMetric, HallucinationMetric, AnswerRelevancyMetric; abstentions carry no context and are skipped.

3.4 · Retrieval alone

Sections beat fixed chunks on Recall@10; the reranker lifts the tail at 5–19 s per search

What the numbers mean

Direct hybrid search (dense + BM25, RRF k = 60) on the questions with PDF gold pages. Recall@k counts gold pages covered by the top-k returned units; recall at matched tokens gives every setup the same text budget, so bigger units cannot win by returning more text.

In production the single hybrid search on the question text gives Recall@5 / @10 of 0.636 / 0.768, while the agent's searches and lookups together reach 0.931 of the gold sources (v2_2026-10-18_0903/summary.md). A v1 parity test of Pinecone against the local dense matrix gave identical Recall@5 / @10 / @20 and MRR; that path was removed with v2.

4 · Reading the results

Why v2 ships at 0.85, what was rejected, and how much is noise

4.1Why v2 is in production although v1 best scored higher

The 0.925 was measured on a laptop. Its one ingredient that does not survive hosting is the cross-encoder reranker: 15–23 s per search on a 2-vCPU server, two to four searches per question, so it cannot meet the 30-second rule. Hosted v1 without it scored 0.85. Under the same constraint v2 matches the 0.85 and improves what staff see: groundedness 0.937 against 0.878, 17/20 answers with no unsupported claim against 15/20, no incorrect answer, cached repeats in under a second.

Two ways to recover the 0.925 remain open: the reranker at depth 50 on a Graviton3 or GPU box (about +$115/month), or Sonnet as v2's primary model (one line in config.yaml).

4.2The reranker inside v2

Retried on 2026-10-18 on the production hardware. bge-reranker-large at depth 50 took 19 s per search on CPU: 5 answers went over 30 s and 6 of 20 questions ended in provider timeouts, with 0.821 on the 14 that answered. bge-reranker-base at the depth the CPU can afford (20) scored 0.70 against 0.85 without it. So v2 ships with search.rerank: null; the code path stays behind the flag.

4.3The live safety gate (rejected)

On 2026-10-17 the agent submitted atomic claims, each checked deterministically against only the pages it cited; one failing direct claim made the whole answer abstain. The gated agent scored 0.300 (4 / 4 / 12), answering 7 of 19 answerable questions, against 0.875 (15 / 5 / 0) and 19 of 19 ungated; the gate removed the 2 unsupported claims the ungated run showed. Decision: the in-code citation check stays live; every other check runs offline on saved traces, and a test proves no checker is called while answering. Safe / useful / over-abstention entered the harness because of this experiment.

4.4The six partial answers in production

No answer was graded incorrect. Three retrieval misses (Q06, Q07, Q10: the gold page was never returned by any tool) and three omissions with the evidence in hand (Q01, Q13, Q18: recall@10 1.0). The references are shorter than the answers (median 67 against 92 words): the model spends words on clinical detail and drops the reference's secondary points. Per question: examples.html.

4.5Two answer-side experiments, kept as switches, off

The completeness pass (agent.min_lookups, agent.completeness_pass) raised groundedness to 0.942, halved the unsupported-claim rate (2.5% → 1.2%) and fixed Q18, but left accuracy at 0.85 and added 8 s of median latency with one answer over 30 s. The pre-submit checklist with one search per sub-question (agent.prompt_extras) cut unsupported claims to 0 (groundedness 0.957, Safe 20/20) but ended at 0.825. Three of the remaining partials are retrieval misses no prompt can fix; DESIGN.md §5's rule is that accuracy must rise and groundedness and recall must not fall.

Figure-derived answers. Every visual answer is correct, yet visual groundedness is 0.61–0.70: the judge gets the cited page's extracted text. The fix is in measurement (give the judge the figure interpretation) and in the product (cite the figure plus the appendix page); neither changes an answer.

4.6Judge noise on 20 questions

Each question runs once and the agent is not deterministic; one grade step on one question moves the score by 0.025, so a one-question difference between runs is within noise. The passes were unanimous on 18 of 20 grades in the production run. The judge is a Claude model and may share the agent's blind spots; each summary.md prints the lowest-scoring answers and a random correct sample for human spot checks, and DeepEval is the independent cross-check. The re-judged baseline differs from its original judging (0.70 against 0.725), which is why one judging configuration is used for every row of §3.1.

5–6 · Data and re-runs

Every number has a folder; a golden run costs about $5 and must keep three things fixed

  • reports/evals/<run>/: summary.md, results.csv (one row per question: grade and judge reason, groundedness, failure class, retrieval and citation metrics, timing, cost, the full answer, trace_path) and meta.json; the folders kept are exactly those named in §3.
  • traces/<timestamp>-<question-slug>.json: the trace each trace_path points at (milestone runs committed).
  • reports/deepeval/<run>/deepeval.json and deepeval.csv: DeepEval scores and reasons for the ten runs of §3.2.
  • reports/retrieval/<timestamp>/results.md and per_question.csv: the three retrieval-only experiments of §3.4.
  • reports/parsing/VISUAL_EXTRACTION.md, FIGURES.json, PARSE_REPORT.json: parsing and figure extraction.
  • LangSmith project pharmassist: one run per answer with child runs per model call, tool call and retrieval. Access by invitation; not checked in.
  • evidence/: the production live check and screenshots (evidence/README.md).
# from the repository root with the local stack up
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_eval.py --label my_change --deepeval            # golden set, DeepEval scorer
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_eval.py --label my_change_visual --deepeval \
    --golden evals/visual.jsonl --tag visual                                     # figure questions
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_eval.py --label my_change                       # Opus three-pass judge instead
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_eval.py --rejudge reports/evals/<run>           # re-score saved answers
.venv/bin/python scripts/deepeval_score.py --run reports/evals/<run>         # DeepEval on a finished run

A golden run costs about $5 and a visual run about $1.25 including the Opus judge; DeepEval adds about $0.09 per question. Acceptance rule (DESIGN.md §5): golden accuracy ≥ 0.85 with 0 incorrect; Q20 returns the exact no-answer sentence; 0 questions over 30 s; 0 answers without citations; 0 citations failing the in-code check; ≥ 18/20 answers within 60–120 words.

  • Must not change: evals/golden.jsonl and evals/visual.jsonl are frozen; hashes 2664dd7d24fb, 0823626fe0c3 go into every run's meta.json, and a run whose hash differs is not comparable with §3.
  • meta.json also records the prompt hash, models, effort, passes, tool cap, deadline, commit and seed; compare these before comparing numbers, and use --rejudge when the judge changed.
  • The judge model, passes and prompts (docs/PROMPTS.md §2) stay fixed for any row added to §3.1; a different judge gets its own column, as DeepEval does.
  • Run folders under reports/evals/ are never edited after the run.
